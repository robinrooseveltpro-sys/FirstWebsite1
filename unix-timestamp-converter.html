What Is a Unix Timestamp?

A Unix timestamp, also called epoch time, represents the number of seconds elapsed since January 1, 1970 at 00:00:00 UTC, excluding leap seconds. Software systems commonly use timestamps in databases, server logs, APIs, and application development.

Seconds vs. milliseconds

Traditional Unix timestamps commonly use seconds. JavaScript's Date.now() returns milliseconds. Microseconds and nanoseconds are also used by some systems and data formats. The number of digits can help identify the unit, but it is only a heuristic; select the correct unit manually when necessary.

Date.now();

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert a Unix timestamp to a date?

Enter the timestamp in the converter and select Convert Timestamp. The tool displays the corresponding date and time.

How can I tell seconds from milliseconds?

Current timestamps commonly have 10 digits in seconds and 13 digits in milliseconds. Microsecond and nanosecond timestamps often have 16 and 19 digits. These are common patterns, not universal rules.

Does a Unix timestamp change with the timezone?

No. The timestamp identifies an instant. The displayed calendar date and time change depending on the timezone used to format that instant.

Can I convert dates back into Unix timestamps?

Yes. Use the Date to Unix Timestamp section, choose the date and timezone interpretation, and convert it.

Can I convert multiple timestamps at once?

Yes. Paste one timestamp or date per line in the batch converter and select Convert All. You can export successfully converted entries as a CSV file.

Is this Unix timestamp converter free?

Yes. The calculations run in your browser. No account or conversion API is required.
