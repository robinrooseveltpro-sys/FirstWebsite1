What Is a UTM Builder?

A UTM builder is a campaign URL generator that adds tracking parameters to a normal destination link. Marketers use these parameters to help identify which source, channel, campaign, or creative was associated with a website visit in an analytics platform.

For example, you might use different tagged URLs for a newsletter, a paid search advertisement, and a social media post even when all three send visitors to the same landing page. The links identify the campaign details attached to each visit, assuming the destination website has appropriate analytics configured.

How to Use This Free UTM Builder

  1. Enter the full destination URL, including the page path.
  2. Choose a campaign source such as Google, a newsletter, or a social platform.
  3. Enter the marketing medium, such as email, cpc, or paid_social.
  4. Add a campaign name that you will use consistently in reports.
  5. Optionally add a campaign term, content label, campaign ID, or advanced parameter.
  6. Review the generated URL, then copy it into the appropriate campaign link field.

Before publishing an advertisement or email, test that the generated link opens the correct page and that the parameters are still present after any redirects.

Understanding the Main UTM Parameters

utm_source — Campaign source

Identifies where the traffic came from, such as a search platform, social network, partner, or newsletter. Choose source names that distinguish the platforms you need to compare.

utm_medium — Campaign medium

Describes the marketing channel or method. Examples can include email, cpc, display, affiliate, paid_social, and referral. Use conventions that match your reporting and advertising setup.

utm_campaign — Campaign name

Identifies the campaign itself, such as a product launch, seasonal promotion, or a named advertising campaign. Reuse the same campaign value for links belonging to that same campaign.

utm_term — Campaign term

Often used to identify paid keywords or targeting terms. Use it when that information is meaningful to the campaign and reporting method.

utm_content — Campaign content

Helps distinguish links or creative variations, such as a top banner versus a footer link, or two different ad creatives in the same campaign.

Additional campaign parameters

Other parameters include utm_id and utm_source_platform. The utm_creative_format and utm_marketing_tactic parameters may also be useful in some setups, but reporting support can differ across analytics tools. Refer to the documentation for the system you use.

See the official Google Analytics documentation on URL builders and UTM parameters for details.

UTM URL Example

Imagine your website is promoting a summer sale through an email newsletter. A tagged destination URL could look like this:

https://example.com/summer-sale?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=summer-sale

In this example, the source identifies the newsletter, the medium identifies email, and the campaign identifies the summer sale. The values are examples only; use the source, medium, and campaign names that accurately describe your own activity.

UTM Naming Best Practices

  • Be consistent: do not alternate randomly between different names for the same platform or campaign.
  • Use a naming convention: agree on a format for source, medium, campaign and creative values.
  • Prefer lowercase values: campaign values with different capitalization can be grouped separately by analytics tools.
  • Choose informative names: use campaign labels that are understandable to the people who read your reports.
  • Do not use personal information: avoid putting names, email addresses, customer IDs, or other confidential data in URL parameters.
  • Check the finished link: confirm the destination and review query parameters before launching the campaign.

Do UTM Parameters Automatically Track Clicks?

No. UTM parameters label a URL; they are not a standalone click-tracking system. Your destination website needs a suitable analytics setup to collect campaign visits and present them in reports. This tool does not track clicks, shorten URLs, send campaign data to advertising platforms, or verify analytics installation.

Keep in mind that a URL can be copied, shared, redirected, or stripped of query parameters by another system. Test your finished link and confirm that campaign data appears as expected in your own analytics reports.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is a UTM builder?

A UTM builder adds campaign parameters to a destination URL. Those parameters can help an analytics platform identify the source, medium, campaign, and other details associated with a visit.

2. Is this free UTM builder suitable for Google Analytics 4?

Yes. It creates URLs using common UTM parameters used by Google Analytics, including utm_source, utm_medium, and utm_campaign. Correct reporting also depends on consistent tagging and your analytics configuration.

3. Which UTM parameters should I use?

Google recommends using utm_source, utm_medium, and utm_campaign when manually tagging campaign URLs. Optional parameters include utm_id, utm_term, utm_content and other supported campaign dimensions.

4. Can I add UTM tags to a URL that already contains query parameters?

Yes. Enter the existing page URL and this builder preserves its unrelated query parameters and URL fragment while adding or updating the supported UTM parameters.

5. Do UTM parameters track clicks by themselves?

No. UTM parameters label campaign links. You need an analytics platform configured on the destination website to collect and report campaign traffic.

6. Does this tool shorten URLs?

No. It creates full campaign URLs. It does not use an external link-shortening service, and it does not provide independent click analytics.

7. Should I use lowercase UTM values?

Using a consistent lowercase naming convention can help prevent differently capitalized campaign values from appearing as separate entries in analytics reports. The lowercase option is enabled by default and can be switched off.

8. Where are my saved UTM links stored?

Links you save in the history are stored in this browser's local storage when available. They are not automatically synchronized with other devices, and clearing browser data may remove them. Use Export History CSV to download a copy.
