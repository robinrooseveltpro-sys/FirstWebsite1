3. What should you fix?

Findings are based on public DNS answers and common configuration checks. Confirm the actual sending service before changing records.

Do not paste example records into DNS without checking your provider's instructions. Incorrect SPF, DKIM or DMARC changes can interrupt legitimate email. Your DNS provider, mailbox host and every authorized sending service must be considered before editing records.

Why do emails go to spam or get rejected?

Email authentication helps receiving systems assess whether a message is authorized to use a domain. Misconfigured DNS records can contribute to authentication failures, spoofing exposure and rejected messages. Authentication alone does not determine inbox placement: reputation, recipient policy, message content and other signals also matter.

What does SPF check?

Sender Policy Framework (SPF) publishes which sending hosts are authorized for a domain. This checker looks for the domain's SPF TXT record, detects multiple SPF records, checks the ending mechanism and estimates DNS-lookup-causing terms in the SPF policy and its static include/redirect tree. The estimate is not a full RFC-compliant SPF evaluator.

What does DKIM check?

DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM) uses a selector to identify a public key in DNS. This tool checks a selector you enter at selector._domainkey.example.com. If the selector is unknown or omitted, DKIM is marked as not checked. One missing selector does not prove the domain has no DKIM configuration; providers may use different selectors or CNAME-based setups.

What does DMARC check?

Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance (DMARC) publishes policy at _dmarc.example.com. This tool checks whether a recognizable DMARC TXT record exists and reviews its policy, reporting and percentage tags. A policy of p=none is a monitoring policy, not necessarily a configuration error. Move to enforcement only when legitimate mail sources are understood and aligned.

What does MX check?

Mail exchanger records identify the destination mail servers for a domain. If your domain should receive email, compare the returned MX records with the instructions from your mail provider. A missing MX answer can be intentional for a domain that does not receive email, so the tool should not automatically treat it as proof of a fault.

How should I fix a failed result?

  1. Identify your actual mailbox and email-sending providers.
  2. Compare the returned public DNS values with each provider's current setup instructions.
  3. Resolve duplicate or invalid SPF records without removing required authorized senders.
  4. Use the exact DKIM selector and public-key setup supplied by your email provider.
  5. Start DMARC monitoring if appropriate, study reports, and apply a stricter policy only when ready.
  6. Send a real test message and inspect its authentication results in the recipient's message headers.

Does a green result guarantee email delivery?

No. DNS presence and basic record syntax are only part of email authentication. This browser tool does not verify a particular message's cryptographic DKIM signature, SPF evaluation against its actual sending IP, DMARC alignment for that message, blocklists, provider reputation or final inbox placement.

Frequently asked questions

Why are my emails going to spam?

Possible causes include failed SPF or DKIM authentication, DMARC alignment problems, sender reputation, message content and recipient filtering. This tool checks public DNS configuration but cannot determine the cause for a specific delivered message.

Can I check SPF, DKIM and DMARC for free?

Yes. Enter your sending domain to look up SPF and DMARC and inspect its MX records. Provide a DKIM selector to check the corresponding public DNS key record.

What does an SPF record beginning with v=spf1 mean?

It identifies an SPF policy. The mechanisms and qualifiers that follow specify how the policy evaluates permitted and non-permitted senders. Do not copy another domain's SPF record without adapting it to your own authorized sending systems.

Why does this tool need a DKIM selector?

DKIM public keys are published under selector-specific DNS names. The selector is normally provided by the email service or shown in the DKIM-Signature header of a real message.

Is p=none in DMARC a failure?

Not automatically. It requests monitoring rather than quarantine or rejection. It can be useful while identifying legitimate email sources, but it does not enforce protection against messages that fail DMARC.

Can a correct DNS configuration still have email delivery problems?

Yes. A DNS checker cannot evaluate every factor that affects inbox placement or a specific message. Inspect the recipient's authentication results, sending reputation, applicable provider requirements and mail server responses as well.
