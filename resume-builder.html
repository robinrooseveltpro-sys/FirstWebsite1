What this tool is, and what it is not

This is a form that formats your own resume content into a clean layout and prints it to PDF. It does not write your resume. It does not invent job titles, dates, or bullet points. Every word in the output comes from what you typed.

That is deliberate. The AI resume builders (Rezi, Zety, Kickresume, and the rest) generate content from a job description, and the result usually reads like every other AI resume. Recruiters have gotten good at spotting them. A resume where you wrote the bullets yourself, laid out in a clean template, is a better artifact than a resume where an AI wrote filler you had to edit anyway.

The other thing this tool does not do is ask for your email. Every major free resume builder gates the PDF download behind an account. That gate exists so the tool can send you job alerts, upsell a premium template, or sell the email list. This one prints straight to PDF from the browser, no account, no watermark on the output.

Why the layout choices are the three they are

Classic uses a serif font (Georgia or Times fallback), a centered name, and underlined section headers. This is the format most applicant tracking systems parse correctly without hesitation, and it is the one to pick when you are applying through a portal that screens resumes algorithmically.

Modern uses a sans-serif font, an accent color for the section titles, and a colored rule under the contact block. This is the format to pick when a human is reading the resume first, which is more common at smaller companies and in creative fields where the resume is one of many signals.

Compact tightens the spacing and reduces the font size slightly, so a dense resume fits on one page without the reader feeling like they are reading a wall. This is the format for people with eight or more years of experience who need to fit everything and still look readable.

What actually goes in a resume in 2026

The summary is optional. A three line summary helps if you are changing careers or your most recent role does not describe what you want to do next. If your most recent role is exactly what you want to do, the summary is a paragraph the recruiter skips to get to the experience. Cut it and give the experience more room.

Bullets beat paragraphs. A bullet point starting with an action verb (Led, Built, Reduced, Shipped) reads faster than a paragraph. Two bullets per role is the minimum. Four to six is the sweet spot. Anything above six means you are describing the job, not your impact in it.

Numbers matter, but only if they are real. "Reduced checkout abandonment by 18 percent" is a real bullet if you measured it. "Reduced checkout abandonment significantly" is a real bullet if you did not. Do not invent a number to make a bullet sound impressive. Recruiters ask about numbers in interviews, and the answer "I estimated it" ends that conversation quickly.

Dates should be month and year, not just year. "March 2022 to June 2024" reads more clearly than "2022 to 2024," and it avoids the ambiguity of whether you were at the job for two years or one and a half.

Applicant tracking systems and why layout matters

Most large companies run resumes through an applicant tracking system (ATS) before a human reads them. The ATS parses the resume into structured fields: name, contact, work history, education, skills. If the layout breaks the parser, the human never sees the resume.

What breaks parsers: multi-column layouts, text in images, headers and footers with critical information, unusual section names like "Where I Have Worked" instead of "Experience," tables, and text boxes. What survives: single-column layouts, standard section names, plain text, and readable fonts.

The Classic template in this tool is built to survive ATS parsing. It is single column, uses standard section names, does not put content in tables, and does not use images. That is why it is the default. The Modern and Compact templates are also single column and standard named, but the accent color in Modern and the background blocks in Compact are slightly riskier if the ATS is old. If you are applying to a large company and do not know their system, use Classic.

Printing to PDF properly

The Print button uses the browser's native print dialog. On Chrome and Edge, the destination dropdown includes "Save as PDF." On Firefox, "Print to File" produces a PDF. On Safari, "Save as PDF" is in the PDF dropdown at the bottom left.

Before you save, check three settings in the dialog. Set margins to "Default" or "Minimum" and not "None," because "None" clips the resume at the page edge. Turn off "Headers and footers" so the browser does not add the URL and date. And if you have a multi-page resume, check the page count in the preview before saving.

The paper is sized for US Letter by default, which is what most US companies expect. If you are applying in Europe or Asia, the browser's print dialog lets you switch to A4, and the layout adjusts because it uses relative units throughout.

Basics

Does this tool write my resume for me?

No. It formats content you type into it. There is no AI in this tool, no content generation, and no suggestion engine. Every word in the output came from you. That is by design, because AI-generated resume bullets tend to read like every other AI-generated resume bullet, and recruiters have gotten good at spotting them.

Do I need to create an account?

No. There is no account, no email field, no password. You fill in the form, print the PDF, and close the tab. The tool does not know who you are and does not store anything on a server. It does save a draft in your browser's local storage so you do not lose your work if you refresh the page by accident.

Is this really free?

Yes. There is no paid tier, no premium template, no upsell. The page has ad slots, and the ads are the only revenue, but the tool itself is fully functional without paying anything.

Can I use this for a cover letter too?

Not directly. This is a resume builder, and the layout is designed for a resume. A cover letter wants a different structure (salutation, body paragraphs, sign-off) that this tool does not produce. If you want a plain cover letter PDF, the browser print dialog on any text editor does the job.

Content

How many work positions should I include?

Enough to cover the last eight to ten years, or three to four roles, whichever is shorter. Older roles from a previous career can be condensed into a single "Earlier experience" line if they are not relevant to the job you are applying for. Listing every job you have ever had dilutes the resume and pushes the relevant experience off the first page.

Should I include a summary if I have strong experience?

Often no. If your most recent role is exactly what you want to do next, the summary is a paragraph the recruiter skips to get to the experience. Cut it and use the space for a fourth bullet on your current role. Include the summary if you are changing careers, returning after a break, or your most recent title does not describe the work you want to do.

How long should each bullet point be?

One to two lines. A bullet longer than two lines is a paragraph in disguise and should be split or cut. The action verb goes first, then what you did, then the result if there is one. "Led the checkout redesign that cut abandonment 18 percent" is two lines and complete. "Worked on various aspects of the checkout flow including design, research, and testing" is a paragraph pretending to be a bullet.

What if I am a recent graduate with no work experience?

Lead with education, then a projects or coursework section, then any internship or part-time work. The skills section carries a lot of weight when you are early in your career. Do not pad the resume with high school jobs unrelated to your field just to fill the page. A short, dense resume reads better than a long, thin one.

Layout and ATS

Which template should I pick?

Classic for anything going through an applicant tracking system, which is most large companies. Modern if a human is reading the resume first and you want it to stand out slightly. Compact if you have eight or more years of experience and need to fit everything on one page. If you are not sure, Classic is the safe default.

Does this tool produce an ATS-friendly resume?

The Classic template does. It is single column, uses standard section names, does not put content in tables, and does not use images. The Modern and Compact templates are also single column and standard named, but their accent colors and background blocks are slightly riskier if the ATS is old. If you are applying to a company whose system you do not know, use Classic.

Why does the resume look different when I print it?

The preview is styled for the screen and the print stylesheet strips the borders, shadows, and paper background. What is left is the resume content with its typography intact. If the printed output looks smaller than expected, check the print dialog's scale setting and make sure it is at 100 percent, not "Fit to page."

Privacy

Does my resume get uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything happens in the browser. The form state and resume content never leave your device. There is no server call, no analytics event tied to your resume content, and no third party that sees what you typed. If you want to verify this, open the browser's developer tools, clear the Network tab, and fill in the form. No requests appear.

What is saved in local storage?

The form values, so if you refresh the page or close the tab and come back, your draft is still there. The saved data stays in your browser and is not transmitted. If you share the device, use the Reset button before you leave to clear the draft from that browser.

Is it safe to put my phone number and address in the form?

Yes, because the data does not leave your browser. The only place it goes is the PDF you print, which is the point of the tool. If you are uncomfortable typing the details into any web page, you can also print the resume from a local text editor after copying the preview, but the form is the point and the data stays on the device.

Export

How do I save the resume as a PDF?

Click Print or Print / Save PDF. In the browser's print dialog, change the destination to "Save as PDF" (Chrome, Edge) or "Print to File" (Firefox). Set margins to Default or Minimum, turn off headers and footers, and save. The resulting PDF has no watermark and no page numbers added by the tool.

What does Download HTML do?

It saves the current resume as a self-contained HTML file you can open in any browser or email to someone. This is useful if you want to send the resume to a recruiter who prefers HTML, or if you want to edit it later without the form. The downloaded file includes the resume content and the styling for the current template.

Does the PDF have a watermark or footer?

No. The print stylesheet hides every part of the page except the resume itself. There is no "Made with" badge, no page footer, and no tool branding anywhere in the PDF. If your browser's print dialog adds a header or footer with the URL, turn off "Headers and footers" in the print dialog before saving.
