About this site

One developer. Free tools. No investors to please.

I'm Rabin. ConvertToToolsOnline is what I work on in the evenings and on weekends — a slow-growing collection of small browser tools that solve the exact problems I kept hitting myself. It is not a startup, and there is no team behind it. Just a laptop, a cheap VPS, and an increasingly stubborn refusal to put a login wall in front of a file converter.

Why this exists

It started with a PDF that would not shrink.

A few years ago I was applying for something that only accepted PDF uploads under 2 MB. The document was 6 MB, mostly because of a scanned signature someone had inserted as a photo. Every "free" online compressor I tried wanted either an account, a credit card, or a watermark on the output. One of them sent me an email three days later asking why I hadn't upgraded. That was the moment the idea clicked — the tools are not hard. The business models around them are just hostile.

ConvertToToolsOnline is my attempt at the opposite. Every tool opens the moment the page loads. Nothing asks for your email. If a file has to be processed on a server, the page says so before you upload. If it runs in your browser, it runs in your browser. That is the entire contract.

Over time, the site grew from one tool to the list you see on the homepage. Some of them, like the DNS lookup and the HAR file viewer, exist purely because I needed them while debugging something at work. Others — the image compressor, the PDF tools — are there because people kept searching for them and I figured I could build a cleaner version.

How it actually runs

The rules I try to keep, even when nobody is watching.

These are not marketing promises. They are the small decisions that keep the site honest — and, not coincidentally, the ones that keep it out of trouble with search engines and ad networks.

01

No account, ever

You should not have to trade an email address for a file converter. If a tool ever needs a login, it will not be listed here.

02

Say where the file goes

Browser-based tools are labelled as such. Anything that uploads a file to a server says so on its own page, in plain English, before you click.

03

No dark-pattern ads

Ads sit in labelled slots and never disguise themselves as buttons, download links, or "continue" prompts. If you see one that does, it is a bug — tell me.

04

No selling of data

Files you upload are not kept after processing and not sold to anyone. The privacy page explains exactly what is stored and for how long.

05

No SEO spam pages

If a tool is listed, it is a real tool. No "coming soon" placeholders, no 500-word filler pages written to rank for a keyword with nothing behind them.

06

Breakages get fixed

Reports get read in batches — sometimes a week late. But they get read. Every broken tool that has been reported here has been fixed eventually.

Transparency

How the site pays for itself.

Hosting, the domain renewal, and the occasional paid API for the DNS and email tools cost money. Nothing here comes from a VC, and I have no plans to add a subscription. The bill is covered by three things, in roughly this order:

1. Small display ads. The dashed rectangles with the word "Advertisement" above them. They are served by Google AdSense and a couple of smaller networks, only after you have consented. They are not the reason the site exists, but they are the reason it can keep existing without a paywall.

2. Reader donations. There is a support page. Some people send a coffee's worth of money after a tool saves them an afternoon. It adds up more than you would expect, and it is genuinely appreciated.

3. Occasional sponsored placements. If a developer tool I actually use wants one labelled slot on the homepage, I will sell it at a fixed rate. It is clearly marked as a sponsor block, and I turn down anything that does not belong on a utility site.

That is the whole revenue model. No affiliate links inside the tools, no data brokers, no "premium" tier. If that ever changes, this page will change first, before anything else on the site.
